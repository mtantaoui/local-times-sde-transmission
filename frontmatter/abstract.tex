\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

This thesis studies one-dimensional stochastic differential equations
involving the local time of the unknown process, together with the partial
differential equations with transmission condition associated with them.

We first recall the construction and the main properties of the local time
of a continuous semimartingale: the Tanaka and It\^o--Tanaka formulas, the
occupation times formula, the regularity of local times in the space
variable, down-crossing approximations and symmetric local times.

We then study the time-inhomogeneous equation
$X_t = x + B_t + \int_0^t \alpha(s)\, dL_s$, where $L$ is the local time of
$X$ at $0$, and prove pathwise uniqueness when $\alpha \leqslant 1/2$,
following Le Gall's method; the result is extended to equations with general
diffusion and drift coefficients. Next, following Le Gall, we consider
equations involving the local times of the unknown process at all levels,
for which we present existence and pathwise uniqueness results, limit
theorems and comparison theorems.

After recalling the probabilistic representation of the solutions of the
heat equation and the Feynman--Kac formula, we finally study the skew
Brownian motion: its construction by random flipping of the excursions of a
reflected Brownian motion, the joint law of the process and its local time,
its transition density, and the parabolic partial differential equation
with transmission condition that it solves.
